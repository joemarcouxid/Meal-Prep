What the hand sizes mean

  • Cupped hand Roughly what fits in one cupped palm. Rice, pasta, cereal, potatoes.
  • Palm The size and thickness of your own palm, fingers not counted. Chicken, beef, fish.
  • Fist One closed fist. Fruit and vegetables.
  • Thumb The length and width of your thumb. Butter, oil, peanut butter, cheese.

Your hands scale with you, which is the whole point of measuring this way. A 15-year-old and an 18-year-old get different amounts from the same instruction, and neither one has to count anything.

What a real meal looks like

Four things. If three of them are on the plate, you're fine.

  • Carbs Rice, pasta, bread, bagels, potatoes, fruit. This is the part people skip. Don't.
  • Protein Chicken, eggs, yogurt, milk, turkey, beef, beans.
  • Produce Whatever fruit or vegetable you'll actually finish.
  • Fluid Water. Add an electrolyte drink when it's hot or you sweat a lot.

    Why the app says what it says

    Match the food to the work

    How much you eat, and when, should track what you're actually doing that day. A rest day and a hard session are not the same job, and a 6 × 400 workout is not the same job as block starts.

    Don't leave long gaps

    Youth athlete guidance suggests eating roughly every three hours, so that a long school day doesn't quietly build an energy deficit inside a single day.

    Under-fueling costs you

    Eating too little over time hurts recovery, bone health, hormones, immune function and speed. It is not a shortcut to getting faster.

    Hands, not numbers

    Portions are given in hand sizes because hands grow as the athlete does. Nothing here needs weighing, counting or logging.

    Food before supplements

    Real food does the job. Supplements are unregulated, and athletes have lost eligibility over banned substances that were never printed on the label.

    Floors, not targets

    Every portion here is a minimum. Growth costs energy on top of training, and teenagers burn more per pound of body weight than adults do.
