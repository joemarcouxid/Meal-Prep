What a real meal looks like

Four things. If three of them are on the plate, you're fine.

  • Carbs Rice, pasta, bread, bagels, potatoes, fruit. This is the part people skip. Don't.
  • Protein Chicken, eggs, yogurt, milk, turkey, beef, beans.
  • Produce Whatever fruit or vegetable you'll actually finish.
  • Fluid Water. Add an electrolyte drink when it's hot or you sweat a lot.

Meet day

One rule above all others: nothing new. If you haven't eaten it before a practice, don't eat it before a race.

  1. 3–4 hours out Bagel with jam, two eggs, banana, yogurt, water. Your real meal of the day.
  2. 60–90 min out Banana, applesauce, pretzels, or half a bagel. Small and simple.
  3. Under 90 min between races Sports drink plus applesauce, banana, crackers, or pretzels.
  4. 2+ hours between races Small turkey sandwich, a bagel, or a modest chicken and rice.
  5. After your last race Chocolate milk and a banana, or anything with carbs and protein.
  6. Within 2 hours A full meal. Carbs, protein, produce, fluids.

Why the app says what it says

Match the food to the work

How much you eat, and when, should track what you're actually doing that day. A hard sprint session and a rest day are not the same job.

Don't leave long gaps

Youth athlete guidance keeps coming back to eating regularly. A seven-hour gap between lunch and dinner is why practice feels awful.

Under-fueling costs you

Eating too little over time hurts recovery, bone health, hormones, and speed. It is not a shortcut to being faster.

Food before supplements

Real food does the job. Supplements are optional, unregulated, and can carry banned substances that cost athletes eligibility.

If you're dealing with ongoing tiredness, repeat injuries, dizziness, times that keep sliding, weight changing without you meaning it to, or periods that have gotten irregular or stopped, tell a parent, your coach, or a doctor.
